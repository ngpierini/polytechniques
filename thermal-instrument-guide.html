Running DSC, TGA and DMA

The standard methods, the settings the literature uses, and how to run them in TA Instruments TRIOS and Mettler Toledo STARe

How to use this guide

The thermal analysis page explains what DSC, TGA and DMA measure and how to read the curves. This page is the bench companion to it: what settings to use, why, and how to put them into the two software packages most polymer labs run, TRIOS (TA Instruments Discovery DSC, TGA and DMA 850) and STARe (Mettler Toledo DSC 3, TGA/DSC 3+ and DMA 1). The settings come from the current ASTM and ISO methods and from the manufacturers’ own training material and application notes; each is cited where it is used.

Three cautions apply throughout. Menu and segment names change between software versions, so treat the step lists as a map rather than a script. Your lab’s standard operating procedure and the instrument’s safety instructions take precedence over anything here. And a method from a material standard (a specification for your product) overrides the general starting points given below. PolyTechniques is not affiliated with either manufacturer.

Before any run: calibration and verification

A measurement is only as good as the last calibration made under the same conditions: same pan type, purge gas, flow and heating rate. Calibrate when the instrument is installed, moved, or serviced, and whenever a check standard drifts; verify routinely with a reference material run as an ordinary sample. Calibration measures the error; adjustment changes the instrument’s parameters to remove it, and only trained users should accept an adjustment.

DSC: a standard heat–cool–heat run

Nearly every polymer DSC measurement starts from the same program, and the two governing methods, ASTM D3418-21 and ISO 11357 (parts 1 to 3), agree on its shape. The settings below are the defaults to start from; change them only for a reason you can write down.

SettingStarting pointWhy, and the standard’s wording
Sample mass5–10 mgD3418-21: about 5 mg for melting and crystallization, 10 mg for Tg, which is a weaker signal. ISO 11357-3:2025 prefers 5–10 mg of polymer. Cut a flat piece that sits on the pan floor; do not grind a semicrystalline sample, which changes it.
PanCrimped aluminium, with a matched empty referenceUse a hermetic pan when moisture or volatiles must stay in. Tzero is a pan family, not a seal: the lid decides whether it is hermetic, and a pinhole lid is vented. Mettler’s 40 µL aluminium crucible is the usual default; aluminium is limited to about 600 °C.
PurgeDry N2, 50 mL/minTA’s usual setting; Mettler examples often use 40 mL/min. D3418 asks for 10–50 mL/min held to ±5. Use the same gas and flow as the calibration.
Heating rate10 K/min (20 is also standard)Both methods allow 10 or 20 K/min; D3418 specifies 10 K/min when results are disputed. Faster gives a bigger signal and a higher apparent Tg.
Upper temperatureAbout 30 K above the end of meltingEnough to erase the crystals the sample arrived with; hold 5 min. Check the decomposition temperature by TGA first, and stay well below it. For an amorphous polymer choose a safe temperature above Tg.
Cooling10 K/min to at least 50 K below the crystallization peakAnd to 50 K below Tg if you want Tg on the reheat; hold 5 min. Record the cooling curve: crystallization on cooling is a result too.
Report fromThe second heatThe first heat describes the sample as received; the second describes the material after a known history. Report both if the history matters to your question.

The standard way to measure a semicrystalline polymer. The first heat records the sample as received, processing history and all, and melts it; the hold erases the crystals it arrived with; a controlled cool gives every sample the same history; the second heat is the one quoted as the material’s own. Heat to about 30 K above the end of melting, and cool to at least 50 K below the crystallization peak, and below Tg if you want Tg on the reheat. The whole program takes 90 minutes at 10 K/min.

Program as described in ASTM D3418-21 and ISO 11357-3:2025 (both allow 10 or 20 K/min; D3418 uses 10 K/min for disputes). Segment names as in TA Instruments TRIOS; Mettler Toledo STARe builds the same method from dynamic and isothermal segments. Temperatures are for a PET-like sample.

Preparing the sample

  1. Tare an empty pan and lid on a microbalance, add the sample, and record the sample-only mass to 0.01 mg.
  2. Press the sample flat against the pan floor so heat reaches all of it; crimp or seal the lid with the press and die that match the pan.
  3. Prepare an empty reference pan of the same type and lid. If you weigh pans (Tzero calibration uses this), record the empty pan and lid masses too.
  4. Inspect the crimp: a domed lid or a sample on the rim gives a poor baseline and can contaminate the cell.

On TA Instruments: TRIOS (Discovery DSC)

  1. Check the instrument is idle, the cooler is running, the purge is on, and a calibration for your pan and gas is active.
  2. In the Experiment view, enter the sample name, the sample mass, the pan type (for example Tzero aluminium) and the tray positions of sample and reference. Set the file name and folder.
  3. In Procedure, choose a custom test and add segments: Equilibrate at the start temperature, Ramp to the upper temperature, Isothermal for the hold, Ramp down, Isothermal, Ramp up again. A Mark end of cycle between heats lets TRIOS split the data into separate steps. Save the procedure to reuse it.
  4. Load the pans (or let the autosampler do it), copy the run to the running queue, and press Start.
  5. To analyse, open the file, pick the second-heat step, select the curve, and use Analysis: Glass transition (onset, midpoint, end), Peak integration (choose a baseline type and the limits), or Onset point. Save, then export to Excel or CSV.

Legacy Q-series instruments run on Advantage (Experiment → Summary for sample details, Procedure → Editor for segments) and are analysed in Universal Analysis 2000; TRIOS can open those files.

On Mettler Toledo: STARe (DSC 3)

  1. Check the module is ready, the cooler and gas controller are on, and the adjustment (FlexCal) for your crucible and gas is current.
  2. Open the Method window, pick the technique and crucible (for example 40 µL aluminium), and build the program from segments: Dynamic for every heating or cooling, Isothermal for every hold. Set the purge gas and flow for the segments. Save the method.
  3. In the Experiment window, choose the method, enter sample name and mass, select the module, and click Send Experiment. This puts it in the module’s queue; it does not necessarily start it.
  4. With the sample robot, place the crucibles in the positions you entered and let autostart run them. Manually, put the sample on the left (S) sensor and the reference on the right (R), close the lid and confirm.
  5. To analyse, open the curve in the Evaluation window, choose the segment, frame the effect, and apply Glass transition, Integration (choose a baseline type) or Onset. Save the evaluation, then export the data as text.

Heat-flow sign: STARe can show either the ICTA convention (exotherm up) or its opposite, set in the Install window. Check which your installation uses and label every plot.

Reading the curve

A first heat of quenched, mostly amorphous PET, read the way the software reads it. The glass transition is a step, not a peak. Above it the chains can move, so the sample crystallizes during the scan (the exotherm, up), and those crystals then melt with everything else (the endotherm, down). Each peak is integrated against a straight baseline between limits chosen on flat curve either side, and the areas are reported per gram of sample. Only the difference between them is crystallinity the sample had before the scan.

Model curve: Tg step Δcp = 0.30 J/(g K) and Gaussian peaks of 24.0 and 39.0 J/g, which the integrations recover. ΔHm0 = 140 J/g for PET (TA Instruments TN048, after Wunderlich). For a filled or blended sample divide by the polymer’s mass fraction as well. Melting is reported as the peak temperature for polymers (ISO 11357-3); calibration standards use the extrapolated onset.

PolymerΔHm0, 100% crystalline
Polyethylene293 J/g
Polypropylene207 J/g
Poly(ethylene terephthalate)140 J/g
Polyamide 6230 J/g
Poly(lactic acid)93.7 J/g

Values from TA Instruments note TN048 (after Wunderlich), and for PLA the widely used literature value. They are estimates for an idealised crystal, not certified constants; cite the one you used.

Which glass transition temperature?

One glass transition, five numbers. On a step with an enthalpy-relaxation peak, the kind an aged or slowly cooled glass shows on reheating, the standard constructions spread over several kelvin, and the fictive temperature lands below the midpoints because the overshoot is extra enthalpy recovered on heating. A DSC Tg is therefore incomplete without its construction, its scan (first or second heat) and its rate. TRIOS offers onset, end, inflection, half height, half width and fictive temperature; STARe offers its own bisector midpoint, DIN, ASTM/IEC and Richardson. Pick one, report it, and use it for every sample you compare.

Constructions computed on a model step (Δcp = 0.30 J/(g K)) with an overshoot. ASTM E1356-25 and D3418-21 prefer the midpoint; ISO 11357-2:2020 prefers equal areas. Software options from TA Instruments TRIOS help and Mettler Toledo’s note on glass-transition evaluation (HB401). Plotted as heat capacity, endotherm up, so the step rises.

ASTM D3418-21, E1356-25The midpoint (half the step between the extrapolated baselines), unless another is stated
ISO 11357-2:2020Prefers equal areas (the fictive temperature); also allows half step height and inflection

TGA: stability and composition

TGA has two standard jobs. A stability run heats the sample in nitrogen and reports where mass loss begins; a compositional run (ASTM E1131) uses nitrogen and then air to split a compound into volatiles, polymer, carbon and ash. ISO 11358-1 covers the general conditions, and E2550 the stability reading.

SettingStarting pointWhy
Sample mass10–20 mgThin and spread out, so heat and gas reach it evenly; a big lump decomposes late and in stages.
CrucibleOpen platinum, or aluminaPlatinum is reusable but alloys with metals and some fillers; alumina suits inorganic or corrosive samples but is porous. Aluminium pans only to about 600 °C.
Heating rate10 or 20 K/minEvery temperature on a TGA curve rises with heating rate, so compare samples only at the same rate.
GasN2 for the sample, N2 for the balanceOn TA Q50/Q500 standard furnaces the documented flows are 40 mL/min to the balance and 60 mL/min to the sample. Keep the balance purge inert even when the sample gas switches to air.
Compositional programN2 to 600 °C, switch to air, continue to 750 °CThe thermoplastics example in ASTM E1131-20; E1131-25 leaves the switch point to the material. Switch only on a flat plateau, after the polymer has gone.
BlankAn empty-crucible run with the same programSubtract it to remove buoyancy drift, especially for small mass losses (ISO 11358-1).

A compositional run on a filled rubber compound, the classic use of TGA. Under nitrogen the volatile and organic fractions leave in order of volatility: moisture, then process oil, then the polymer itself, while carbon black survives. Switching the purge to air at 600 °C burns the carbon off, and whatever remains is mineral ash. Read each fraction from the plateaus either side of its step, using the derivative (DTG) to place the boundaries where steps overlap. One caution the standard itself makes: the air step is all combustible residue, so char left by the polymer counts as “carbon black” too.

Program after the thermoplastics example in ASTM E1131-20 (10 °C/min, N2 to 600 °C, then air); the current E1131-25 leaves the switch temperature to the material. Curve computed from first-order Arrhenius kinetics for each component (moisture 1, oil 14, polymer 52, carbon black 25, ash 8 wt%); the step sizes read off the curve recover them to within half a percent. Keep the balance purge inert when the sample gas switches to air.

On TA Instruments: TRIOS (Discovery TGA)

  1. Put the clean, empty crucible in its tray position and run Instrument → Tare for that position.
  2. Add the sample to the same crucible and return it to the same position; enter the sample name, pan type and file name. The balance measures the mass.
  3. In Procedure, add Equilibrate at the start temperature, Ramp to 600 °C, Select gas to change to air (check which gas port is which on your installation), and Ramp to 750 °C.
  4. Start the run; the instrument loads the crucible and closes the furnace. Watch the first minutes for a stable mass.
  5. Analyse with Step transition on the mass curve for each step’s onset, end and mass change, and plot the first derivative to place boundaries between overlapping steps.

Where fitted (standard on the TGA 5500, an upgrade on the 550), the High resolution ramp slows the heating automatically while mass is being lost, separating steps that a constant rate merges.

On Mettler Toledo: STARe (TGA/DSC 3+)

  1. Configure the gas controller in the Install window: with a GC 10 the software selects between two gases and you set the flow; a GC 200 also regulates the flow.
  2. Build the method from Dynamic segments: nitrogen to 600 °C, then a segment with the method gas changed to air up to 750 °C. A change of gas between segments switches the valves.
  3. Run a blank curve with an empty crucible and the same method, for subtraction.
  4. Tare the empty crucible as your configuration requires, add the sample, enter its name and mass in the Experiment window, and Send Experiment.
  5. Evaluate with Step horizontal or Step tangential on each mass loss, and Math → 1st derivative for the DTG curve.

Reading the curve

  • Say which decomposition temperature. The extrapolated onset, the temperature at 5% loss (T5%) and the DTG peak (Tmax) can differ by tens of kelvin. Report the definition with the number.
  • The air step is combustible residue, not automatically carbon black. Char left by the polymer burns there too; E1131 says so itself.
  • For kinetics, run several heating rates. An activation energy from one ramp is not reliable. ASTM E1641 (Ozawa–Flynn–Wall) and the ICTAC Kinetics Committee recommendations (2014, and 2020 for multistep reactions) describe how.

DMA: a temperature ramp at 1 Hz

The workhorse DMA experiment heats a clamped specimen while oscillating it at one frequency, recording storage modulus E′, loss modulus E″ and tan δ. ASTM D4065 is the general practice; the geometry methods are D5418 (dual cantilever), D5023 (three-point bending) and D5026 (tension); ASTM E1640 assigns Tg. ISO 6721 is the international equivalent.

SettingStarting pointWhy
Frequency1 HzThe E1640 default and the near-universal choice, so results compare across labs. Each decade of frequency moves Tg by roughly 5–7 K.
Heating rate1 °C/min for E1640; 2–3 °C/min is common practiceA bar of polymer lags the furnace; faster ramps push every transition upward. Report the rate you used.
AmplitudeInside the linear viscoelastic regionRun a strain sweep first at the starting temperature and pick an amplitude where the modulus does not depend on it. E1640 recommends strain below 1% and no more than 5%.
Preload, force trackEnough static force to keep tension or contactTension and three-point bending need a static force larger than the oscillating one; on the DMA 850, force track (for example 125%) keeps that ratio as the specimen softens.
TgE′ onset (E1640), and say which axisReport E″ and tan δ peaks as well if you like, but name each: they are different temperatures.

Three glass transitions from one DMA run. The storage modulus E′ (black, log scale) falls three decades; the loss modulus E″ (green) peaks partway down; and tan δ = E″/E′ (violet, right axis) peaks last, because E′ is still falling after E″ has turned over. The order is always the same, onset before E″ peak before tan δ peak, but the gaps are not a constant that can be subtracted: they depend on how broad the transition is. ASTM E1640 takes the onset, which is the temperature at which the material starts to lose stiffness and the conservative number for design.

Computed from a Cole–Cole relaxation (α = 0.7, glassy 2.2 GPa, rubbery 20 MPa, plus a background tan δ of 0.012) with a Vogel–Fulcher time scale moving about 6 K per decade of frequency, at 1 Hz; E1640 onset by the two-tangent construction on log E′. ASTM E1640-23 specifies 1 Hz and 1 °C/min. For a measured comparison, TA Instruments’ polycarbonate example (TA082) gives 141.8, 147.1 and 151.5 °C at 1 Hz and 3 °C/min.

On TA Instruments: TRIOS (DMA 850)

  1. Install the clamp, select the same clamp and mode in TRIOS, and run its calibration (mass, zero, compliance as the wizard asks).
  2. Measure the specimen with calipers (length or span, width, thickness) and enter the dimensions; mount it centrally and tighten as the clamp instructions say. Place the thermocouple close to the specimen without touching it.
  3. Run an Oscillation → Strain sweep at the start temperature and 1 Hz; choose an amplitude on the flat part of the modulus curve.
  4. Set up a Temperature ramp at 1 Hz with that amplitude, the preload and force track your geometry needs, and the temperature range. Use the multifrequency ramp to see the frequency shift.
  5. Press Measure to preview contact and the oscillation; if it looks right, close the furnace and Start. Analyse with onset on E′ and peak analysis on E″ and tan δ.

On Mettler Toledo: STARe (DMA 1)

  1. Choose the deformation mode and fit the matching clamp; measure the specimen and mount it by the clamp’s instructions.
  2. In the method, set the temperature program, frequency (or frequencies), force or displacement amplitude and any offset. Find the linear range with an amplitude sweep first.
  3. Enter the sample name and geometry in the Experiment window, select the DMA module and send it.
  4. Evaluate in the Evaluation window: onset of the E′ drop and the peaks of E″ and tan δ, each with its frequency.

Clamp calibration and force-track settings from the DMA 850 do not transfer to the DMA 1; follow its own procedure.

Write it up: a methods-section builder

Fill in what you actually ran and copy the paragraph into your notebook or paper. Everything a reader needs to repeat the measurement goes in: instrument, sample mass and pan, gas and flow, program, and how each number was read.

Reporting checklist

  • Every technique: instrument model and software version, calibration standard and date, sample mass or dimensions and how it was prepared, pan or clamp, gas and flow, full temperature program with holds.
  • DSC: which heat each number comes from; the Tg construction; whether Tm is peak or onset; the integration limits and baseline; the ΔHm0 used for crystallinity; and the plot direction (exo up or endo up).
  • TGA: crucible, sample and balance flows, the switch temperature and gas, the decomposition-temperature definition, and whether a blank was subtracted.
  • DMA: geometry and span, strain or displacement amplitude and how it was chosen, preload or force track, frequency, ramp rate, and which Tg marker on which axis scale.
