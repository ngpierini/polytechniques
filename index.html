About this toolkit

PolyTechniques is a set of calculators and reference guides for people who actually run polymerizations. It was built by a synthetic polymer chemist around the calculations that come up repeatedly at the bench — targeting a molecular weight before weighing anything out, working out why the GPC disagrees with that target, deciding whether a copolymer will drift away from its feed composition, and reading a DSC or DMA trace once the material exists.

The tools are deliberately not black boxes. Each one states the equation it uses, the assumptions behind it, and the conditions under which it stops being valid, because a number produced without those is worse than no number at all. Where a parameter is genuinely uncertain — a Flory–Huggins χ, a termination rate constant, a reactivity ratio — the pages say so and show how much the answer moves when it changes, rather than presenting a fitted guess to four decimal places.

Alongside the calculators sits a curated structure library of 968 polymers, searchable by drawn repeat unit, by name or trade name, or by CAS number, and laid out for reading by chemical family. Every structure, registry number and property in it is checked in continuous integration against the same rules, because a reference table that is quietly wrong is the most expensive kind of tool to own.

Everything works offline once loaded, and nothing you type into a calculator or a search box leaves your browser. The one exception is asking the search to recognize a drawn structure from a photograph, which has to send that image to be interpreted — the privacy page sets out exactly what happens to it, and what the other two paths for reading an image do instead. The toolkit is free to use; if something here is wrong, or if there is a calculation you keep doing by hand that belongs on this site, the contact page is the place to say so.
